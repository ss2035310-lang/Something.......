Getting started with AI is harder than it looks

There are plenty of AI tools. Knowing where to begin is the tricky part.

Not knowing which tools to try

New AI tools appear all the time, and it is hard to tell which ones are worth a student’s attention.

Too many options

Long lists and comparisons can leave you with more open tabs than answers.

Staring at an empty prompt box

Not knowing what to type is one of the most common reasons beginners give up early.

Jumping between random tools

Without a simple routine, it is easy to hop between apps instead of building a workflow.

A practical starting guide, not a magic solution

This eBook organises 25 tools into six categories, explains what each one is for in plain language, gives you something to try with each, and shows how to combine them into simple routines. You still do the learning. The eBook gives you a clearer map to start from.

The all-rounder that explains almost anything
Preview
CategoryAI assistants
Best forExplaining concepts, brainstorming, practice questions
Free planFree plan with usage limits and an account recommended. The most capable models are usually limited on the free tier.

Try this “Explain photosynthesis as if I am new to biology. Then ask me five questions, one at a time, and correct my answers.”

Watch out: It can state wrong facts and invent references with total confidence. Verify anything you plan to cite.

CategoryResearch tools
Best forFinding and comparing academic papers quickly
Free planFreemium: a limited number of free credits. Paid plans exist if you outgrow it.

Try this “Does spaced repetition improve long-term retention in university students?”

Watch out: Even good academic AI tools sometimes misdescribe findings. Always open the original paper before citing it.
